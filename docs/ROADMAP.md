# ROADMAP

## Structural Intelligence Compass

### From a 12-Point Compass to Open Structural Exploration

> **The purpose of this roadmap is not to fill the repository. It is to test whether the Compass remains useful when the territory becomes larger.**

---

# 1. Roadmap Purpose

The **Structural Intelligence Compass** is intentionally small.

Its development should not be measured primarily by:

* number of papers;
* number of figures;
* number of algorithms;
* number of Compass points;
* size of the repository.

The preferred measures of progress are:

* clarity of the twelve research directions;
* quality of counterexamples;
* identification of validity boundaries;
* cross-domain usefulness;
* successful structural decomposition;
* discovery of Residual Intelligence;
* experimental validation;
* simplification of computational structure;
* independent extension by other researchers.

The repository therefore follows an **evidence-driven and boundary-aware roadmap**.

# 7. Phase V — Search for Residual Intelligence

## Objective

Identify stable computational residuals rather than forcing all intelligent behavior into the existing framework.

A residual becomes particularly interesting when it is:

* structurally identifiable;
* repeatedly observed;
* difficult to represent using the current basis;
* present across more than one task;
* not merely an implementation limitation;
* not easily reducible to an existing operator.

Possible interpretations include:

### Representation Residual

The current structures cannot naturally represent the required information.

### Organizational Residual

The information can be represented but not organized effectively.

### Generative Residual

The system cannot preserve the necessary core while producing required variation.

### Runtime Residual

The appropriate computation exists but cannot be reliably activated or coordinated.

### Decision Residual

Available structures do not support the required choice or trigger.

### Validation Residual

The system cannot determine whether its result remains structurally valid.

### Primitive Residual

A genuinely new first-class computational structure may be required.

The last category should be reached only after simpler explanations have been excluded.

# 8. Phase VI — Multi-Perspective Function Tunnel Experiments

## Objective

Explore whether multiple Function Tunnels can generate stronger runtime trigger conditions than a single perspective.

One candidate experimental progression is:

### Stage A

**Single-Valued X–Y–M**

A local state maps to one observed decision and measure.

### Stage B

**Multi-Valued X–Y–M**

Similar or related states accumulate multiple decisions and outcomes.

### Stage C

**Cross-Outcome Structural Comparison**

Compare cases associated with different outcomes.

### Stage D

**Two-Way CCC**

Identify:

* preserved structure;
* subtracted structure;
* distinguishing structure.

### Stage E

**Trigger Synthesis**

Generate candidate structural conditions under which runtime behavior should switch.

### Stage F

**Validation**

Compare generated triggers against:

* baseline decisions;
* historical outcomes;
* alternative Function Tunnels;
* future runtime evidence.

### Research Question

> **Can structural comparison across multiple runtime perspectives generate better triggers than direct statistical selection alone?**

This direction may provide a concrete experimental bridge among:

**Function Tunnels + Two-Way CCC + X–Y–M + PDOS/PDOR + Local Node Intelligence**

# 9. Phase VII — Structural Extrapolation

## Objective

Develop clearer distinctions among different forms of extrapolation.

### Metric Extrapolation

How far is the new observation from previous observations?

### Statistical Extrapolation

How reliable is a fitted relationship outside its observed region?

### Structural Extrapolation

Which important structural relations remain preserved?

### Causal Extrapolation

Does the relevant mechanism continue to operate?

### Runtime Extrapolation

Does the current decision remain valid under the new runtime condition?

A key experimental class should deliberately compare cases with:

**high metric similarity + low structural continuity**

against cases with:

**low metric similarity + high structural continuity**.

This can help separate similarity-based confidence from structure-based confidence.

---

# 10. Phase VIII — Multi-Axis Confidence

## Objective

Investigate whether intelligent systems should represent confidence as more than one scalar probability.

Candidate dimensions include:

* metric confidence;
* statistical confidence;
* structural confidence;
* causal confidence;
* runtime validation confidence;
* perspective agreement;
* boundary proximity.

A future system might therefore distinguish:

> **I have little historical precedent for this case, but the relevant structural invariant appears strongly preserved.**

from:

> **This case looks statistically familiar, but a critical structural boundary condition has changed.**

Such distinctions may be particularly important for:

* scientific reasoning;
* engineering;
* market triggering;
* planning;
* autonomous systems;
* Hybrid AI.

# 12. Phase X — Digital Brain Stress Test

## Objective

Use Digital Brain architecture as an integration test rather than as proof of the Compass.

A candidate Digital Brain should be examined for:

### Organization

Can large information spaces be differentially organized?

### Memory

Can local and global experience be retained without requiring universal full connectivity?

### Runtime Addressing

Can computation reach the appropriate local structure efficiently?

### Local Intelligence

Can different nodes use different computational mechanisms?

### Generation

Can invariant cores be preserved while controlled variation is produced?

### Triggering

Can runtime conditions activate appropriate computational paths?

### Switching

Can authority move when conditions change?

### Feedback

Can outcomes modify future decisions?

### Validation

Can the system evaluate its own runtime results?

### Boundary Intelligence

Can the system identify when existing knowledge or computation is insufficient?

### Open Learning

Can new structures and perspectives be incorporated without rebuilding the entire system?

The Digital Brain is therefore treated as a **composition and coverage test** for the structural basis.

# 13. What This Roadmap Does Not Require

The repository does not require:

* one paper for every Compass point;
* continuous expansion of the document count;
* a new algorithm for every research question;
* a new primitive for every unexplained behavior;
* a single unified intelligence architecture;
* replacement of LLMs;
* replacement of World Models;
* proof that the twelve points are complete;
* proof that the proposed structural basis is minimal;
* a final theory of AGI.

Some research directions may eventually belong in other repositories.

Some may remain questions.

Some may disappear.

That is acceptable.

---

# 14. Repository Growth Rule

New material should be added only when it does at least one of the following:

1. clarifies an existing Compass point;
2. establishes a meaningful connection among points;
3. provides experimental evidence;
4. identifies a significant boundary;
5. provides a strong counterexample;
6. exposes Residual Intelligence;
7. introduces a genuinely distinct structural primitive;
8. materially improves the use of the Compass as a research instrument.

Repository growth is not itself a research objective.

> **Do not add text merely because there is room for another document.**

---

# 15. Revision Rule

The Compass should be revised when evidence requires revision.

Possible future changes include:

* renaming a point;
* redefining a point;
* merging points;
* splitting points;
* adding a new direction;
* removing an unnecessary direction;
* revising the proposed structural basis;
* replacing an operator;
* changing the interpretation of Hybrid Intelligence.

A stable release means:

> **stable enough to use**

not:

> **closed against future correction**.

# 17. Long-Term Research Question

The deepest long-term question of this repository is not:

> **Can Structural Intelligence explain everything?**

It is:

> **How much intelligent computation can be constructed from a small, reusable structural basis while explicitly preserving the boundaries of what that basis can know and do?**

Two outcomes are equally valuable.

### Outcome A — Strong Coverage

A small structural basis continues to explain increasingly broad intelligent behavior.

This strengthens the case for **Operator Economy** and minimal Digital Brain architectures.

### Outcome B — Stable Residual

A class of intelligent behavior repeatedly resists natural decomposition using the current basis.

This provides evidence for a missing structural mechanism.

Either outcome advances the research.
