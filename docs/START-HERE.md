## Structural Intelligence Compass

> **A compass, not a closed map.**

The **Structural Intelligence Compass** is a compact, open research framework for examining intelligence from twelve structural directions.

It is not intended to be a complete theory of intelligence.

It is not a declaration that intelligence has exactly twelve fundamental dimensions.

It is not a claim that Structural Intelligence, Large Language Models, Hybrid AI, or future AGI can possess absolute truth.

It is an invitation to examine intelligence through a small set of questions that appear increasingly difficult to avoid.

---

# 1. Start with One Question

Before reading the repository, consider a simple question:

> **When an intelligent system produces a highly successful answer, what exactly has been established?**

Has the system discovered truth?

Has it produced a useful answer within one computational perspective?

Has it interpolated from previous experience?

Has it preserved a causal or structural invariant?

Has it bypassed a difficult computational path?

Has it compressed previous calling paths into reusable capability?

Does the answer remain valid outside the conditions under which it was produced?

And what happens when another perspective produces a different answer?

These questions motivate the Structural Intelligence Compass.

---

# 2. The 12-Point Compass

The Compass contains four groups of three points.

## I. Three Fundamental Challenges

### Point 1 — Relative Truth vs. Absolute Truth

A successful computational perspective should not automatically be identified with complete truth.

Different representations, observations, models, and Function Tunnels may expose different parts of an underlying structure.

---

### Point 2 — The Boundary of Extrapolation

Metric proximity and structural validity are not the same.

A statistically distant case may preserve an important causal structure.

A metrically similar case may violate a decisive structural condition.

---

### Point 3 — The Boundary of Computational Difficulty

Bypassing one computational difficulty does not imply that computational difficulty itself has disappeared.

Difficulty may be reduced, transformed, relocated, or remain structurally unavoidable.

---

## II. Three Structural Foundations

### Point 4 — Differential-Tree Structures

Organize large spaces through progressively meaningful differences.

### Point 5 — Calling-Graph Structures

Represent potential computational relationships, reachability, authority, and runtime organization.

### Point 6 — Calling-Path Folding

Compress large amounts of experienced computational structure into reusable runtime capability.

Large Language Models provide an important example of this perspective.

---

## III. Three Engineering Directions

### Point 7 — PDOS / PDOR

Policy-driven organization, routing, localized computation, X–Y–M memory, feedback, and validation.

### Point 8 — Core-Preserved Generation

Preserve essential invariants while allowing controlled generative variation.

### Point 9 — Local Node Intelligence

Allow different local nodes to use different intelligence mechanisms according to their computational requirements.

---

## IV. Three Research Frontiers

### Point 10 — Multi-Perspective Function Tunnels

Use multiple computational perspectives to identify preserved structures, structural differences, and potentially new trigger conditions.

### Point 11 — Structural Extrapolation and Confidence

Study how confidence should change when metric continuity, causal continuity, and structural continuity disagree.

### Point 12 — Hybrid Intelligence

Organize LLMs, Structural Intelligence, World Models, tools, causal models, simulators, humans, and other computational systems under explicit runtime organization.

And then return to Point 1.

> **Greater intelligence increases coverage. It does not automatically terminate the boundary of knowledge.**

---

# 3. Why Is It a Compass?

The twelve points are not twelve stages of intelligence.

They are not twelve modules that must appear in every AI architecture.

They are directions from which an intelligent system can be examined.

When evaluating a new AI method, for example, the Compass encourages questions such as:

* What perspective does the method expose?
* What does it preserve?
* What does it lose?
* Where does its validity boundary lie?
* What kind of extrapolation does it support?
* Which computational difficulty has actually been reduced?
* How is its knowledge organized?
* What calling structures does it use?
* What has been compressed?
* Where does runtime authority reside?
* How is confidence established?
* What happens when another intelligence mechanism disagrees?

The Compass is therefore intended primarily as a **research navigation instrument**.

# 5. The Four-Paper Logic

The repository follows a deliberately small progression:

> **View → Boundary → Basis → Invitation**

### View

What does the Structural Intelligence landscape currently look like?

### Boundary

What can no single computational perspective legitimately claim?

### Basis

How small might the structural basis of broad intelligence actually be?

### Invitation

Where does the proposed basis fail, and what are we still missing?

The fourth question deliberately remains open.

---

# 6. Three Major Compression Structures

One observation behind this repository is that several broad forms of intelligent computation may be organized around a surprisingly small number of major compression structures.

## Organizational Compression

**Large X-Space → Differential Organization → Local Address**

A large problem space becomes computationally manageable through structural organization.

---

## Core-Preserved Generative Compression

**Large Generative Space → Preserved Core + Controlled Delta**

Generation becomes useful when essential structure survives variation.

---

## Calling-Path Compression

**Large Experience Space → Folded Computational Structure → Runtime Reconstruction**

Previously experienced relationships and computational paths can be compressed into reusable runtime capability.

LLMs provide an important example.

---

These structures do not imply that only three forms of intelligent computation exist.

The research question is more modest:

> **How much intelligent computation can be constructed from a small number of major structural compression mechanisms plus reusable secondary operators?**

---

# 7. Secondary Structural Operators

A small first-class basis does not imply a simple runtime system.

Broad intelligence may emerge through composition with smaller structural mechanisms such as:

* Two-Way CCC;
* Variable-Size Block Index;
* differential trees;
* Function Tunnels;
* Runtime Invariants;
* triggering;
* runtime switching;
* Calling Graphs;
* X–Y–M memory;
* local decision engines;
* feedback;
* validation.

The important distinction is between:

**fundamental structural diversity**

and

**compositional runtime complexity**.

A system may exhibit enormous behavioral complexity while relying on relatively few reusable computational principles.

---

# 8. Do Not Assume the Basis Is Complete

This repository makes no completeness claim.

The inability to currently identify another first-class MET structure is interesting, but it is not proof that another structure does not exist.

The preferred response is therefore not to defend the current basis.

It is to attack it.

Try scientific discovery.

Try robotics.

Try mathematics.

Try market competition.

Try World Models.

Try long-horizon planning.

Try software engineering.

Try biological intelligence.

Try unfamiliar domains.

Then ask:

> **What remains that cannot be naturally represented, organized, compressed, generated, routed, triggered, learned, or validated?**

That remainder is important.

We call it:

## Residual Intelligence

A stable residual may indicate that the current structural basis is incomplete.

Finding such a residual would be a useful result.

Failing to find one after increasingly broad stress testing would also be a useful result.

# 9. The Boundary Remains Open

One of the most important principles of the Compass is that increased intelligence should not be confused with ownership of truth.

Even a highly capable future Hybrid AI may combine:

**LLMs + Structural Intelligence + World Models + Causal Models + Tools + Simulators + Humans + Runtime Validation**

and still operate through finite observations, representations, computational structures, and perspectives.

Therefore:

**More Perspectives ≠ All Perspectives**

**Better Coverage ≠ Complete Coverage**

**Higher Confidence ≠ Absolute Truth**

**General Intelligence ≠ Omniscience**

A mature intelligent system should not merely produce answers.

It should also help represent:

* what it knows;
* from which perspective it knows it;
* under what conditions the knowledge remains valid;
* how strongly it has been validated;
* where extrapolation begins;
* what could invalidate the conclusion;
* what remains unknown.

This capability is referred to here as **Boundary Intelligence**.

---

# 10. The Compass Returns to Its Beginning

Point 12 does not complete the Compass.

It returns to Point 1.

A stronger Hybrid Intelligence creates new capabilities.

New capabilities expose new territory.

New territory reveals new uncertainty.

New perspectives expose new structural differences.

New differences create new questions.

The boundary moves again.

Therefore:

> **Intelligence does not terminate the unknown. It reorganizes the boundary between the known and the unknown.**

The Compass is a loop because intelligence itself operates against an open future.

# 11. How to Use This Repository

There is no requirement to accept the full framework.

Use whichever part is useful.

If you work on LLMs, examine Calling-Path Folding.

If you work on agents, examine Calling Graphs, runtime authority, and triggering.

If you work on World Models, examine perspective, structural extrapolation, and Boundary Intelligence.

If you work on AI Coding, examine Core-Preserved Generation and computational difficulty.

If you work on causal reasoning, examine structural extrapolation.

If you work on knowledge organization, examine differential structures.

If you work on AGI, examine all twelve points — especially the return from Point 12 to Point 1.

And if your research exposes something that does not fit the Compass:

> **That may be more valuable than fitting it.**
